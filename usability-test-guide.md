# Círculo Market — Usability Test Guide

**Version:** 2.0

**Session:** 45 minutes
**Participants:** 5–8 people who already sell occasionally online or through messaging groups

## 1. Objectives

1. Confirm that people understand the difference between `Solo amigos` and `Público con enlace`.
2. Verify that creating and sharing a first catalog feels easier than using a mass marketplace or sending individual messages.
3. Identify whether the trust-based proposition is valuable enough to motivate an invitation.
4. Detect friction in publishing, connecting with a friend, browsing a catalog, and starting a chat.

## 2. Questions to validate

- Can a new user explain the product in their own words after seeing the landing page?
- Do users know who will see a product before publishing it?
- Do they understand that a public link never exposes friends-only products?
- Is “friend” understood as an access relationship rather than a social feed?
- At what moment does the product feel more useful than WhatsApp, Vinted, Wallapop, or Milanuncios?
- What would make someone return after their first listing?

## 3. Participant mix

- 2–3 people who currently sell through mass marketplaces.
- 2–3 people who share items mainly in WhatsApp, Telegram, or social media.
- At least one person who is cautious about publishing personal items publicly.
- Use each participant's real phone when possible.

Test in pairs for the invitation and catalog flows: one participant acts as seller and the other as a friend or buyer.

## 4. Setup

- Two non-admin test accounts with realistic profiles.
- One account starts with no friends or products.
- One account has three products: one `friends`, two `everyone`.
- A valid friend invitation link.
- A public catalog link.
- A product photo ready on the participant's phone.
- Screen recording only with explicit consent.

Reset data between sessions. Never use production personal data.

## 5. Introduction script

> “Hoy vas a probar Círculo Market, una herramienta para vender cosas entre personas conocidas y compartir productos públicamente solo cuando quieras. No estamos evaluándote a ti: estamos evaluando el producto. Piensa en voz alta y cuéntame qué esperas que ocurra antes de cada acción.”

Do not explain the visibility model before the participant encounters it. We need to learn whether the interface explains it.

## 6. Tasks

### Task 1 — Understand the proposition

Start on the public landing page.

> “Mira esta página como si alguien te hubiera enviado el enlace. Cuéntame qué crees que hace el producto y para quién es.”

Observe whether “catalog,” “friends,” and “public link” are understood, which CTA feels natural, and which alternatives the participant mentions.

Success: the participant can explain the core value without facilitator help.

### Task 2 — Create a catalog and publish privately

> “Tienes una lámpara que quieres ofrecer primero a tus amigos por 15 €. Publícala para que solo ellos puedan verla.”

Observe the time to find the publish action, whether price versus gift is clear, whether visibility is understood, and confidence before submitting.

Success: a `friends` listing is submitted without accidental public exposure.

### Task 3 — Invite a friend

> “Quieres que una amiga pueda ver tu catálogo privado. Invítala de la forma que te resulte más natural.”

Observe whether the user understands what the invitation grants, whether WhatsApp feels expected, and whether the post-share state explains what happens next.

Success: the invitation is shared and the participant knows that acceptance creates the relationship.

### Task 4 — Accept an invitation and browse

On the second device/account:

> “Has recibido esta invitación. Acéptala y encuentra la lámpara de tu amiga.”

Observe trust signals, signup/login friction, and whether the user lands somewhere useful after acceptance.

Success: the friendship becomes active and the private product is visible.

### Task 5 — Make one product public

Back on the seller account:

> “La lámpara no se ha vendido. Ahora quieres compartirla en un grupo donde no todos tienen cuenta. Haz únicamente ese producto público.”

Observe whether the user changes the correct listing, whether they expect the whole catalog to become public, and whether sharing explains what an anonymous visitor will see.

Success: only the intended product appears through the public catalog link.

### Task 6 — Contact the seller

> “Te interesa un producto. Pregunta si sigue disponible y acuerda cómo recibirlo.”

Observe time to find the message action, whether chat retains product context, and whether users understand that payment and delivery happen outside the platform.

Success: a conversation is created and both accounts can exchange messages.

### Task 7 — Maintain the catalog

> “La venta ha terminado. Actualiza el catálogo para que nadie más pregunte por el producto.”

Observe whether “vendido” is easier to find than delete and whether the resulting state feels complete.

Success: the product is marked sold and no longer appears as available.

## 7. Post-session interview

1. ¿Qué problema crees que resuelve Círculo Market?
2. ¿Cuándo elegirías “solo amigos” y cuándo un enlace público?
3. ¿A quién invitarías primero?
4. ¿Qué venderías aquí que no publicarías en un marketplace masivo?
5. ¿Qué te impediría volver después de hoy?
6. ¿Pagarías por alguna función? ¿Cuál y por qué?
7. Si pudieras cambiar una sola cosa, ¿cuál sería?

## 8. Measures

- Task completion without help.
- Time to first published product.
- Time to first accepted friend.
- Time to first shared public link.
- Visibility errors or moments of doubt.
- First-click accuracy for each main action.
- Confidence rating from 1–5 after tasks 2 and 5.
- Stated preference versus messaging and mass marketplaces.

## 9. Decision rules

- Any accidental public exposure is a release blocker.
- If fewer than 4 of 5 participants explain both visibility modes correctly, revise terminology before adding features.
- If publishing takes more than three minutes for most participants, simplify the form.
- If fewer than half would invite a real friend, revisit the value proposition and catalog-empty state.
- Prioritize repeated behavioral problems over isolated aesthetic preferences.
